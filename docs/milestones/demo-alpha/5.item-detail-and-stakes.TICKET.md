# Item detail and stakes

**Status:** Complete.

**Type:** Frontend feature plus Realtime. Depends on ticket 4's order list screen (shares its layout mount and `['order', orderId]` query).

**Goal:** The order item detail screen is the only place stakes get committed, adjusted, or withdrawn, and both screens update live across devices without a refresh.

## Decisions
- A stake commit is an upsert (`ON CONFLICT (order_item_id, user_id) DO UPDATE`); a withdrawal is a plain `DELETE`. Both are already permitted by `trg_check_stake_order_is_open` while the order is open — this is the first UI to use an existing allowance, not a new capability.
- Realtime subscribes only to `orders` (`UPDATE`) and `order_items` (`INSERT`, `UPDATE`), filtered by `order_id`, never `event: '*'`, and never `order_item_stakes` directly — a debounced (150ms) invalidation of `['order', orderId]` covers stake changes too, since they roll up into `order_items.quantity`.
- Realtime subscription mounts once in `order/_layout.tsx`, not per-screen, so an update lands correctly whether the viewer is on the list or the detail screen.
- **The stepper adjusts a draft and commits on one press**, rather than writing on every tap. A stake is a commitment other people plan around, and on a tiered item every write reprices every other member's stake and wakes every other device. Stepping to zero turns the commit button into a withdrawal.
- **Members are named by the local part of their email** (`member4`, `admin`), from `syndicate_member_profiles`. That view is the only route to identity, because `order_item_stakes.user_id` references `auth.users` and PostgREST will not embed it.
- **The detail screen draws its own header band**, matching the list's, rather than the navigator's. The countdown comes with it: this is the screen where somebody decides how much to commit, so how long they have belongs in front of them.

## Verification

Driven headless against the local stack and the dev server, not by inspection.

**Two members, two browser contexts, neither refreshed.** Member2 opened item 2's detail and stepped to 2 units; member5 sat on the same screen and never touched it. Member5's page moved on its own: the tier ladder's live rung went from `0–4` to `5–9`, the standing line changed, member2's €43.00 appeared under "Who's in", and **member4's existing stake repriced €74.97 → €64.50** — the all-units retroactive reprice `trg_sync_stake_amounts_on_unit_price` performs, arriving on a device that made no request.

**The draft prices the tier the item would land on.** At 3 of 2,500 with the item on €24.99, a draft of 2 units quoted €43.00 (2 × €21.50) and said "This is the unit that drops everyone to €21.50 each" — not €49.98 at today's price.

**Capacity holds at both ends.** On the 6-pack at 5 of 6, `+` disabled at a draft of 1 with "1 unit is all the room this item has for you", and the draft said "This completes the pack".

**Losing the race is handled without a failed write.** With a draft of 1 armed on the last unit, the admin's stake was inserted out of band. Realtime delivered the fill, the draft clamped to 0, `+` disabled, and the line read "This one is full. Nothing left to take." Forcing the write anyway (an earlier run, before clamping) returned `P0001` and rendered "That didn't land — the table moved while you were deciding. The figures above are current."

**Withdrawal removes the row.** `SELECT count(*)` returned 0 afterwards, the item fell back from 5 to 3, and the price returned to €24.99 on screen.

**A closed order goes read-only live.** The order was closed out of band while the detail screen sat open: with no reload, the badge became "Closed", the countdown and window rule disappeared, the stepper was replaced by read-only text, and the stake list's footer changed from "so far" to "settled".

**Apportionment is visible through the UI.** After close, the filled 6-pack's shares read €16.67 / €16.67 / €8.33 / €8.33 — two members moved up a cent from €16.66 — summing to exactly €50.00, confirmed as `SUM(stake_amount) = 5000`.

**Also checked:** `/order/99` renders "Not on this table" rather than an order-level error; a non-member is told so on the detail URL as well as the list. `pnpm test` 105 passing, `tsc --noEmit` and `pnpm lint` clean. The only console error across every run is the known first-request `PGRST303` 401, already documented in `frontend.md`.
